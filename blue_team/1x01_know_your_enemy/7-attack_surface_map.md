# MedDefense Health Systems: The Attack Surface Map

**Prepared by:** Aïda Sylla, Security Analyst **Prepared for:** James Chen, Deputy CISO **Source material:** Project 0x00 Network Scan Summary and Asset Registry (`7-asset_registry.md`), Control Matrix (`10-complete_control_matrix.md`), Gap Analysis (`12-gap_analysis.md`/`13-reality_check.md`/`15-predecessor_review.md`), and Tasks 0–6 of this project **Purpose:** Map every point where an attacker could attempt to interact with MedDefense's systems, data, or people, distinct from a vulnerability assessment. This document identifies _where to look_; the gaps referenced throughout identify _what is already known to be wrong there_.

## Section 2: Internal Surface (accessible once inside the network)

Every entry in this section carries the same underlying multiplier: **MedDefense's network has no segmentation.** The Project 0x00 network scan confirmed this empirically, "a device on any subnet can reach any other device on any other subnet", which means every item below is not an isolated exposure contained to its own segment, but a fully reachable target from any single point of compromise anywhere in the environment, including a compromised guest device, a phished workstation, or a compromised vendor access path (Task 5).

### Exposed Services

| Asset          | Exposure (port/service)                                                                                | Why It Matters on a Flat Network                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| billing-srv-01 | MySQL, port 3306, reachable from the entire internal network                                           | Any compromised device anywhere in the environment can connect directly to the billing/claims database, bypassing the application layer entirely (GAP-003's sibling finding: The same exposure pattern, confirmed on a second critical database). |
| ehr-db-01      | PostgreSQL, port 5432, reachable from the entire internal network rather than restricted to ehr-srv-01 | Direct, application-bypassing access to the complete patient record database from any point of compromise (GAP-003).                                                                                                                              |

### Management Interfaces

| Asset                                                                  | Exposure                                                               | Why It Matters on a Flat Network                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| NAS-01 (backup)                                                        | Management interface, ports 5000/5001, reachable network-wide          | An attacker does not need to compromise a server to threaten MedDefense's only backup. Direct access to the backup management console is available from anywhere on the network (compounds GAP-006).                                                                                   |
| Medical IoT devices (Philips monitors, BD Alaris pumps, badge readers) | HTTP/HTTPS management interfaces, ports 80/443, reachable network-wide | These interfaces are reachable from any workstation, not just clinical/biomedical management systems, the same exposure that let a real-world comparable attacker (Task 2's BlackReef case studies; Task 1's Report F) pivot from a general foothold directly to patient-care devices. |
| FortiGate administrative interface                                     | Not confirmed to be restricted to a dedicated management network       | Unverified: If the admin GUI is reachable from the general network rather than an isolated management segment, the single device enforcing the entire perimeter's default-deny policy is itself sitting on the network it is meant to protect.                                         |

## Section 3: Human Surface (people who can be targeted)

| Role                                                                                | What They Can Access                                                                          | Why They Are Targetable                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Training/Control Gap (0x00/1x01)                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Clinical staff** (nurses, physicians, technicians)                                | EHR patient records, PACS imaging, medical device interfaces                                  | Security awareness training completion is inconsistent and, at Westside, as low as 58%; clinical workflow demands broad, unrestricted access to patient data by design, and high work-pressure environments reduce vigilance against social engineering (directly demonstrated in Task 4 Scenario 3, a vishing call targeting a nurse).                                                                                        | GAP-012 (incomplete/inconsistent training, no phishing simulation program), GAP-017 (no MFA, a single phished credential grants full EHR access), GAP-022 (the shared PACS login further erodes individual accountability for this group specifically).     |
| **Reception/Registration staff**                                                    | Patient registration systems, registration-level EHR access                                   | The first point of both physical and social contact for anyone entering the building or calling in, the natural target for pretexting, vishing, and tailgating (Task 4 Scenarios 3 and 7); Task 3's "Curious Employee" scenario was specifically a registration clerk.                                                                                                                                                         | GAP-007 (physical access uses the same undifferentiated badge for every role, including reception), GAP-012 (training), GAP-004 (no behavioral monitoring to catch anomalous access by a registration-level account).                                       |
| **IT staff** (12-person department, 3 sites)                                        | Elevated/administrative privileges across Active Directory, network devices, and servers      | A small team covering 3 sites is structurally prone to fatigue and workload-driven shortcuts, directly demonstrated in Task 3 Scenario 5, where an overworked administrator stored AD admin credentials in plaintext to cope with a ticket backlog; IT staff are also high-value phishing targets precisely because compromising one grants broad organizational access (Task 4 Scenario 1 targeted the IT Director directly). | GAP-017 (no MFA on administrative accounts), GAP-025 (no change management process, the exact condition that enabled Scenario 5's risky shortcut), GAP-009 (no periodic access/compliance review).                                                          |
| **Executives** (CEO, CFO, Deputy CISO, IT Director)                                 | Financial approval authority, strategic and organization-wide visibility                      | Explicit BEC targets by virtue of their authority (Task 4 Scenario 2: a spoofed CEO instructing the CFO to wire $85,000); publicly known names and roles (org charts, public bios) make pretexting straightforward to construct; day-to-day involvement in hands-on security practice is typically lower for this group than for technical staff.                                                                              | GAP-017 (no MFA on executive accounts), and no dual-approval/out-of-band verification process currently exists for high-value financial requests (the Administrative Control recommended in Task 4 Scenario 2 does not yet exist as an implemented policy). |
| **External contractors** (MedTech Solutions, Siemens, and other vendors per Task 5) | Varies by vendor, but frequently privileged: direct EHR maintenance access, MRI system access | Entirely outside MedDefense's direct visibility or control: MedDefense cannot enforce its own training standards, monitoring, or credential hygiene on a vendor's own staff, and access is typically granted as standing/broad rather than time-limited and scoped (Task 5 findings).                                                                                                                                          | Every vendor-specific gap identified in Task 5 applies here: no dedicated vendor network segmentation, no confirmed MFA requirement on vendor remote access, and no periodic review of vendor access (GAP-009).                                             |

## Surface Assessment Summary

The **internal surface** represents the greatest risk to MedDefense today. Not because it contains more individual entry points than the external or human surfaces, but because it is the single factor that determines whether a breach anywhere else on this map stays contained or becomes catastrophic. Every scenario examined across this project's first 6 tasks (BlackReef's ransomware lifecycle (Task 2), 5 of 5 insider scenarios (Task 3), all 7 social engineering vectors (Task 4), and every vendor compromise path (Task 5)) converges on the same finding once an initial foothold is achieved: the complete absence of network segmentation gives that foothold unrestricted reach to the EHR database, the domain controllers, the backup infrastructure, and every medical device in the building, regardless of whether the initial entry came from a phished executive, a compromised vendor, a negligent insider's shadow IT device, or a direct external exploit. The external and human surfaces determine _how likely_ MedDefense is to be breached in the first place; the internal surface determines _how bad it gets once that happens_, and on the evidence gathered throughout this project, that second question currently has the worst possible answer.
