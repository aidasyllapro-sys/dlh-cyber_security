# MedDefense Health Systems: The Gap-Threat Correlation

**Prepared by:** Aïda Sylla, Security Analyst **Prepared for:** James Chen, Deputy CISO **Source material:** Project 0x00 Gap Analysis (`12-gap_analysis.md`, `13-reality_check.md`, `15-predecessor_review.md`, 25 gaps), cross-referenced against the Threat Actor Matrix (T6), Kill Chains (T10), and the Three Scenarios (T14) of this project **Purpose:** Recalibrate gap priority by asking not just "how exposed is this asset" (0x00's inward-looking method) but "who would actually exploit this, and does it appear on a real attack path we've now built"; The outward-looking half of the equation.

**2 new gaps formalized in this task:** 2 weaknesses surfaced repeatedly across this project's kill chains and scenarios (T5, T10, T14) without ever being assigned their own 0x00 Gap ID. They are formalized here for the first time:

- **GAP-026: No dedicated vendor-access segmentation or jump-host** (first identified in T5, central to Kill Chain #5 and Scenario 3)
- **GAP-027: No email authentication (DMARC/SPF/DKIM) or out-of-band financial-transaction verification policy** (first identified in T4 Scenario 2, central to Kill Chain #4)

**Methodological note on downgrades:** No gap in this correlation was downgraded. A gap's absence from one of the 5 kill chains or 3 scenarios built in this project is not, by itself, evidence that the underlying risk has decreased Only 5 kill chains and 3 scenarios were constructed, not an exhaustive attack-path enumeration. Where a gap does not appear in any built kill chain or scenario, this is noted explicitly below as a **coverage gap in this project's own scenario-building**, not as grounds to lower its 0x00-assessed risk level.

## 2. The Critical 3

Ranked by raw frequency across the 5 kill chains and 3 scenarios (8 total threat artifacts):

**1. GAP-014 (Flat network, no segmentation) appears in 6 of 8 kill chains/scenarios.** No other gap comes close. This is not a new conclusion. It is the sixth independent confirmation in this project (after T6, T7, T8, T9, T10) that network segmentation is the single highest-leverage control MedDefense could implement, because it is the one weakness that turns nearly every other successful compromise into an organization-wide event.

**2. GAP-003 (EHR database network-wide exposure) appears in 5 of 8.** This gap is the specific mechanism by which GAP-014's flat network actually reaches MedDefense's single most critical asset; closing GAP-003 alone (restricting ehr-db-01 to ehr-srv-01) would break the final step of 5 separate, otherwise-successful attack paths even without full network segmentation.

**3. GAP-004 (No centralized detection) appears in 3 of 5 kill chains, the widest kill-chain spread of any gap (narrowly ahead of GAP-017's 2 of 5).** Every one of MedDefense's most dangerous attack paths depends on going unnoticed for days, not just on gaining access. This gap is why "successful intrusion" and "successful attack" are not the same thing at MedDefense today.

Closing these 3 gaps would not eliminate every threat in this project, but they represent the smallest set of interventions that disrupts the largest number of distinct, evidenced attack paths. Exactly the criterion this task asks for.

## 3. The Surprise

**GAP-012 (Incomplete/inconsistent security awareness training)** was rated **Medium** in the original 0x00 Gap Analysis, on reasoning that training completion — while real and worth fixing — is a "partial control that reduces but does not eliminate risk," placing it below the Critical/High gaps tied directly to a specific technical exposure.

Threat modeling changed this understanding directly. In building Kill Chain #1 and Scenario 1, the project's #1 priority attack path, independently confirmed across 4 separate analyses in this project. **Step 2 is a spear phishing email that succeeds specifically because Sarah Park, the IT Director, has never been through a phishing simulation exercise.** This is not a hypothetical connection: it is a near-exact match to the real scenario already built in Task 4 of this project. GAP-012 is not a soft, generic "people need more training" finding. It is the literal opening move of the single most dangerous, most corroborated threat MedDefense faces. A Medium rating made sense when this gap was evaluated only against its own, isolated impact (a phishing-susceptible workforce). It stops making sense once threat modeling shows that same susceptibility is the specific hinge on which the organization's highest-priority kill chain turns. **GAP-012 is upgraded to High** as a direct result of this correlation exercise, a concrete demonstration of exactly why 0x00's inward-looking risk assessment needed this outward-looking threat correlation to be complete.
