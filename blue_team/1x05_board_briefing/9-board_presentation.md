## Part 1: Board Security Brief

**Current Threat Status.** CISA confirms an active ransomware campaign, Crimson Tide, has compromised 5 regional hospitals in 10 days, 3 within 50 miles of MedDefense Central, using a known, currently exploited vulnerability in our exact firewall model. Our own analysis confirms MedDefense is exposed on 5 of the 7 documented stages of this attack today, and Hospital C, still in active containment 3 days after compromise with the FBI on site, sits close enough that this is not a distant, abstract warning.

**Security Posture Verdict.** Five weeks of work produced a complete, funded, evidence-based strategy with every recommendation traced to a specific gap, a specific vulnerability, and a specific dollar figure, not a generic best-practices checklist. That strategy is well-designed but largely undeployed today: funded is not the same as protected, and this brief does not blur that line anywhere in what follows.

**Emergency Response Summary.** Tonight, before anyone sleeps: verify our firewall's patch status, disable the vulnerable remote-access feature as an interim safeguard, physically disconnect our backup system from the network, and rotate our highest-risk credentials. Tomorrow, pending your approval: renew the $2,400 support contract currently blocking our patch, apply it during a brief, scheduled connectivity window, and verify our endpoint protection is actually active across every server, not merely licensed. This week: accelerate network segmentation and database encryption, both already designed and tested in a controlled environment, into live production use.

**Investment Summary.** This program already secured Board approval for $95,800 of a $120,000 budget across six controls, segmentation, multi-factor authentication, continuous monitoring, backup replication, endpoint protection, and a firewall replacement at our Westside clinic, projected to return a combined $6 million in annual risk reduction once fully deployed. Tonight's request is $2,400, already covered entirely by the $24,200 you already approved and left in reserve; no new budget authorization is required for this specific action tonight. A second, larger, genuinely separate decision, 24/7 security monitoring previously rejected on cost grounds, now sits close to financial breakeven given tonight's updated intelligence and deserves your consideration on its own merits at a future session, not bundled into this emergency request.

**Recommendation.** Approve the $2,400 firewall patch tonight, our highest-return decision produced in five weeks of analysis, and endorse the 72-hour plan as presented without modification. This closes the specific, confirmed door Crimson Tide is using against hospitals matching our profile right now; it does not make MedDefense invulnerable, and this document has not claimed otherwise anywhere across five weeks of work.

## Part 2: The Stakeholder Map

**Dr. Morales (CEO): Patient safety and organizational reputation.** Our own analysis confirms medical device dependencies, EHR integration, imaging, on servers this same attack chain reaches; a successful attack does not just steal data, it can take patient-facing systems offline the way it already has 45 miles from here, with ambulances currently being diverted. Tonight's $2,400 action directly closes the entry point into that chain, before it reaches those systems, not after.

**Robert Kim (CFO): Financial exposure, ROI, cost of inaction.** Tonight's specific ask returns $4,296,600 in avoided expected loss against a $2,400 cost, a 1,790-to-1 return, the clearest financial case this program has produced in five weeks. The cost of inaction is not abstract: the confirmed ransom demands against comparable hospitals this month alone range from $1.2 million to $3.5 million, before counting weeks of lost revenue during recovery.

**Dr. Reeves (Board Chair): Professional recommendation and confidence level.** My recommendation is to approve tonight's action without reservation; the evidence behind it, a named CVE, confirmed active exploitation, and a direct match to our own environment, is as solid as any finding this program has produced. My confidence is lower on the 30-day roadmap succeeding on schedule with our current two-person team, and I am telling you that directly rather than promising a timeline I cannot fully guarantee.

**Thomas Wright (Former banker): Comparison to financial sector security maturity.** Banking-sector peers typically operate mandatory patch cadences, continuous monitoring, and rehearsed incident response, three specific capabilities this assessment confirms MedDefense does not yet have in production, gaps this roadmap targets directly, not gaps we are unaware of. Tonight's action is the kind of baseline patch discipline your former industry would treat as routine, not exceptional, and its absence here is precisely what let this exposure persist unverified until tonight.

**Maria Santos (General Counsel): HIPAA liability, breach notification, insurance status.** This assessment concludes directly that MedDefense could not pass a HIPAA security audit today, with the database encryption gap Crimson Tide is actively exploiting elsewhere as the most citable deficiency. If a breach matching this campaign's pattern occurs, mandatory patient notification and OCR reporting obligations follow immediately; I recommend confirming our current cyber insurance policy limits against the $4.3 million exposure this document quantifies before, not after, we need to rely on it.
