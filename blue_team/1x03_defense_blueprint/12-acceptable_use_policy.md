# MedDefense Health Systems

## Acceptable Use Policy

**Policy Owner:** Deputy CISO **Approved By:** Chief Executive Officer **Effective Date:** [To be inserted upon Board approval] **Review Cycle:** Annual, or upon material change to MedDefense's systems or risk posture **Applies To:** All workforce members, as defined below

---

### 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy defines the acceptable, prohibited, and required behaviors for anyone who accesses MedDefense Health Systems' information systems, network, or facilities. It exists to protect patient safety, protect patient and financial data, and keep MedDefense's clinical operations running without interruption.

This policy applies to every workforce member, including full-time and part-time employees, physicians, contractors, volunteers, and temporary staff, at every MedDefense location (Central, Westside Clinic, and Corporate HQ). It covers every system MedDefense owns or manages: the electronic health record (EHR) system, billing and financial systems, email, the internal network, connected medical devices, and any personal device that connects to MedDefense's network or handles MedDefense data.

---

### 2. Acceptable Use of Systems

MedDefense systems are provided for clinical, administrative, and business purposes related to your role. You may use them to deliver patient care, perform your job duties, and communicate with colleagues, patients, and vendors as your role requires.

Limited, reasonable personal use of email or internet access is permitted during breaks, provided it does not interfere with your duties, consume significant system resources, or violate any other provision of this policy. Access to patient records must always be limited to what is necessary for your specific role and the specific patient interaction in front of you, consistent with the minimum-necessary standard under HIPAA. Browsing records for patients not under your care, including friends, family, or public figures, is a violation of this policy regardless of your intent.

---

### 3. Prohibited Activities

The following are prohibited without prior written authorization from IT Security:

- Connecting any unregistered or unauthorized device to MedDefense's internal network. Unidentified devices have been found on MedDefense's network before; any device not in the current asset inventory will be treated as unauthorized and disconnected on discovery.
- Installing unauthorized software, or disabling, uninstalling, or interfering with any security software installed on a MedDefense system, including antivirus and endpoint protection.
- Sharing your login credentials with another person for any reason, or using an account that is not your own, including shared department logins.
- Attempting to bypass, disable, or work around any security control, including network restrictions, multi-factor authentication, or monitoring tools.
- Storing patient data, financial data, or MedDefense credentials on a personal cloud storage account, personal email account, or any service not approved by IT Security.
- Sending patient health information or financial data by email to an external recipient unless the message is encrypted through an approved method.

---

### 4. Personal Devices and Removable Media

USB drives and other removable storage media are **not permitted** on clinical workstations unless a specific business need has been approved in writing by IT Security. Where an exception is approved, only an IT-issued, encrypted device may be used.

Personal phones and personal laptops may connect only to the designated guest network, never directly to the clinical, administrative, or server network. A personal device must never be used to store, download, or directly access patient data. If your role requires mobile access to MedDefense systems, request an IT-managed device or enrollment in MedDefense's mobile device management program instead.

Any personal or unregistered device found connected to a non-guest network segment will be disconnected immediately and reviewed by IT Security, consistent with MedDefense's asset management practice.

---

### 5. Password and Authentication Requirements

- Passwords must be at least 12 characters long and unique to each MedDefense account. Do not reuse a password from any other service.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required for all remote and VPN access, all administrative accounts, and all systems containing patient health information.
- Never share your MFA device, code, or credentials with another person, including a coworker covering your shift.
- Accounts inactive for 45 consecutive days will be automatically disabled and must be reactivated through IT Security.

---

### 6. Data Handling

MedDefense classifies data into the following categories. Handle each accordingly:

- **Patient Medical Records (Restricted):** Access on a need-to-know basis only. Must be encrypted at rest and in transit. Never store on a personal device or unencrypted removable media. Never email externally without encryption.
- **Financial and Billing Data (Confidential):** Access limited to Billing, Finance, and authorized administrative roles. Same encryption and storage requirements as Restricted data.
- **Internal Administrative Data:** Scheduling, internal communications, and operational documents. Share only within MedDefense unless explicitly authorized for external release.
- **Credentials:** Never write down a password where others can see it, never share it, and never store it in an unencrypted file or note. If you believe your credentials have been compromised, change them immediately and report it to IT Security the same day.

---

### 7. Monitoring and Enforcement

MedDefense monitors network traffic, system access logs, email, and connected devices for security purposes. You should have no expectation of privacy when using MedDefense systems or network resources.

Violations of this policy are reviewed by IT Security, in coordination with Human Resources and, for clinical staff, your Department Head. A first, minor violation typically results in a documented warning and retraining. Repeated or willful violations may result in suspension of system access, disciplinary action up to and including termination, and, where patient data is involved, a formal HIPAA breach assessment that may carry separate regulatory reporting obligations.

**Emergency exception:** during a genuine, immediate patient emergency, clinical staff may take an action that would otherwise violate a non-safety-critical provision of this policy (for example, using a colleague's already-logged-in session to avoid a delay in care) without prior approval. Any such action must be documented and reported to your Department Head within 24 hours for review. Patient safety comes first; this policy exists to protect patients, not to stand in the way of caring for one in front of you.
