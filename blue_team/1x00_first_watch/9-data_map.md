# MedDefense Health Systems: Data Map - Classification, Lifecycle and Protection Gaps

**Prepared by:** Aïda Sylla, Security Analyst **Prepared for:** James Chen, Deputy CISO (for Legal Counsel) **Source material:** All prior project findings (Tasks 0–8), consolidated by data category rather than by system **Purpose:** Trace MedDefense's sensitive data across its 3 lifecycle states (at rest, in transit, and in use) to identify where protection is missing at each step, per Legal's request regarding the organization's protected health information (documented in this task's context as covering over 50,000 patients), financial data, and employee records.

**Classification scale used:**

|Level|Definition|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Restricted|Highest sensitivity. Unauthorized access causes severe harm. Regulatory penalties.|Patient medical records, SSNs, credit card numbers|
|Confidential|Sensitive internal information. Unauthorized access causes significant harm.|Employee salaries, strategic plans, vendor contracts|
|Internal|Not for public disclosure but limited impact if exposed.|Internal memos, org charts, meeting notes|
|Public|Intended for public consumption. No harm if disclosed.|Website content, public phone numbers|

## Data Risk Summary

MedDefense's most significant data protection weakness is not any single database or application. It is the exposure of **System Credentials in the "at rest" and "in use" states inside the unlocked network closet** (Task 3, Observation 2). Every other Restricted-classification data category in this map (patient medical records, imaging data, billing data, and medical device data) ultimately depends on the network infrastructure that these posted credentials control; an actor who reads that laminated sheet does not need to individually attack the EHR, PACS, or billing systems, because switch-level access on this flat, unsegmented network is sufficient to intercept or redirect traffic for all of them simultaneously. This gap is disproportionate to its classification precisely because a Restricted-level secret, meant to protect the most sensitive systems in the organization, is currently protected by nothing more than an unlocked door, the widest possible gap between what the data is worth and how it is actually protected.
