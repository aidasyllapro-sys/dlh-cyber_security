MedDefense's security today relies almost entirely on locks, not alarms: we can slow attackers down in some places, but we have very limited ability to notice one getting in. That gap already let a hidden intrusion run undetected on a financial server for weeks earlier this year.

Our single biggest exposure is that every computer, medical device, and server at Central shares one open network with no internal walls between them. A compromised nurse's workstation can reach the systems holding patient records and running infusion pumps directly, the same weakness behind recent hospital ransomware attacks making national news. If exploited here, we could face a multi-day shutdown of patient records, cancelled procedures, and mandatory breach notification to every affected patient.

Three actions come first. One: deploy basic monitoring so we see an attack in progress instead of discovering it by accident - $20,000, underway within 30 days. Two: wall off medical devices and core servers from general computers - $35,000, first phase within 3–6 months. Three: require a second login step for remote and administrative access, and automatically cut off access the moment an employee leaves - under $15,000, complete within 30 days.

This year's $120,000 plan costs roughly 2% of what one comparable hospital breach in our own research cost to recover from - about $5 million. Prevention here is dramatically cheaper than recovery.

Without these 3 actions, the question is not if MedDefense faces an incident like this. It's when, and how much worse ours will be for having no way to see it coming.
