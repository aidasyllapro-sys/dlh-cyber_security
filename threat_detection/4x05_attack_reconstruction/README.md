HEALTHBANE Campaign: Attack Reconstruction

James Chen stands at the whiteboard with the whole security operations team in the room and Dr. Patricia Morales on video. The board shows a timeline in five colored segments, with gaps between each one. Six weeks of genuinely excellent work sit in those segments - phishing dissection, network forensics, threat intelligence mapping, malware triage, proactive threat hunting - and none of it connects cleanly. The network timeline's timestamps don't match the SIEM hunt's. An IOC from the phishing investigation shows up in the malware analysis but never in the network capture. The 4x02 ATT&CK mapping calls a technique "inferred"; the 4x04 hunt confirmed a different variant of that same technique with direct evidence. Then the incident response evidence lands: a scheduled task on WS-RECV-03 firing every night at 02:00 that no SIEM rule ever looked for, compressed archives of patient records staged in a temp directory, and a firewall session to an IP address that appears in none of the five prior investigations. The board meets in one week. They want one report, one timeline, one recommendation package - not five more status updates.

This project turns five independent investigations plus the new IR evidence into a single reconstruction, where what the evidence does *not* show matters as much as what it does. Every claim here cites its evidence - source file, timestamp, host, user account - because an uncited claim isn't a finding, it's speculation, and every claim carries one of three confidence levels: CONFIRMED (direct evidence from at least two independent sources), PROBABLE (strong evidence from one source with supporting context), or POSSIBLE (technique logic supports it, but the direct evidence is thin or ambiguous). An inventory catalogs every source and how much it can be trusted before any of it gets interpreted. Cross-source correlation surfaces convergence and contradiction instead of smoothing over either, and when two sources disagree on timing or sequence, both get documented, the probable cause gets named - timezone, clock skew, a collection gap - and one source gets picked as authoritative, with the reasoning stated. The attack timeline is rebuilt on an explicit line between what's confirmed by direct correlation and what's merely inferred from technique logic. The ATT&CK mapping gets real confidence annotations instead of a single reassuring percentage. The impact assessment separates confirmed data exposure from potential exposure from exposure that was actually prevented - because the HIPAA notification decision depends on getting that distinction right. Every deliverable is a script producing a structured, source-cited artifact: nothing reaches the final report without a finding, a timestamp and a reliability rating behind it.

See `0-evidence_index.sh` onward for the full deliverable set.
