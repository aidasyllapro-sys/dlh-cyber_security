## Reclassification Notes (Initial vs Final)

- **E2, E3, E5, E7** all carried the triage label `SUSPICIOUS`, which does not exist in the final taxonomy — deeper analysis did not reverse any of these four (none turned out to be benign), it resolved each into a specific, more actionable category:
  - **E2 and E5** moved to `PHISHING-TARGETED` because task 3's social engineering analysis rated both `TARGETED`: each pretext referenced a real, role-specific process (nursing shift systems for E2, an accounts-payable invoicing relationship for E5) that required reconnaissance beyond just a name and email address.
  - **E3 and E7** moved to `PHISHING-OPPORTUNISTIC` because task 3 rated both `SEMI-TARGETED`: each is a reused, generic template (a Microsoft account alert, an HR open-enrollment notice) personalized only by inserting the recipient's name and email, not by referencing anything specific to their job function.
- **E1, E4, E6, E8** kept the same category from triage to final verdict — deeper analysis (header, authentication, and where applicable content review) confirmed the initial read in each case rather than changing it.

## Triage Accuracy Assessment

Measuring accuracy at the level that matters operationally — whether the initial triage sent each email in the right *direction* (malicious/spam vs. legitimate) — the task 0 triage was correct for **8 out of 8 emails (100%)**: no email initially marked `LEGITIMATE` or `SPAM` was later found to be a missed phishing threat, and no email initially marked `SUSPICIOUS` was later cleared as benign.

What changed between triage and final verdict was resolution, not direction: triage's 3-label scheme (`SPAM`/`SUSPICIOUS`/`LEGITIMATE`) is intentionally coarse and fast, built to prioritize investigation order rather than to produce a final answer. Four of the eight `SUSPICIOUS` calls needed the deeper header, authentication, and content analysis in tasks 1–4 to split into `PHISHING-TARGETED` versus `PHISHING-OPPORTUNISTIC` — a distinction triage-level indicators alone could not reliably support, since both categories present with failed authentication and a credential-harvesting link at first glance. This confirms the triage methodology is reliable for initial prioritization (getting P1-URGENT on E2 immediately, for instance) but was never intended, and should not be relied upon, to stand in for the full investigation.
